# Zotero Journal Club

Produce an evidence-grounded, editable PPTX and associated analysis, source manifest and QA report. Default to Chinese, graduate audience, 15–20 minutes and roughly 12–16 slides, adjusted to the source and user instructions. One paper is the primary workflow; never invent missing evidence.

1. Read [source collection](references/sources.md). Discover available Zotero tools; search by title, DOI, item key or link, resolve to the parent and disambiguate by title, authors, year and DOI. Read metadata, attachments, notes and annotations with their actual coverage. Treat search results as candidates, not exact matches. If the current selection is unavailable, request an identifier or PDF; do not claim access to it. Reuse existing MinerU text; obtain key figures from the explicit PDF attachment through the available figure tool or local, visually inspected PDF crops. Preserve attachment identity and relative image paths. Compare figures, formulae and numbers against the PDF. Distinguish paper evidence, human or AI-generated notes and your inference. Do not modify the library or send private materials to a third party.
2. Read [analysis and presentation](references/presentation.md). Analyze the paper before planning, and construct a claim-to-source map with PDF page (one-based), printed page if known, figure/panel, table, equation and section. Turn useful notes and annotations into reading questions, check them against the paper, and connect them to slide explanations or discussion. Organize around basic information; problem/method/evidence; and concrete lessons/discussion, budgeting most speaking time for method and results. Journal metrics are optional: include JIF/JCR or CAS classifications when requested or relevant to the supplied template, verify each from its own authoritative source with year and citation, and do not delay the talk for unavailable metrics.
3. Read [visual quality](references/visual-quality.md). A supplied finished presentation or configured `quality_reference` sets the expected visual finish for future talks using this skill. Resolve it independently of the explicitly supplied/configured template with `scripts/resolve_template.py`; render and inspect its complete visual grammar. Respect the actual template when selected; otherwise use an intentionally authored academic design, with the bundled `academic-blue` preset as a baseline. Plan varied, content-appropriate pages with clear chapter/page hierarchy, aligned original figures and captions, editable method relationships, readable tables and concise takeaways. Preserve original files, theme and masters where used; never flatten them or leave sample content. Add substantive notes with transitions, evidence reading and duration.
4. Build in the chosen output directory `output/<paper-id>/`: `presentation.pptx`, `paper_analysis.md`, `source_manifest.md`, `qa_report.md` and `assets/` as needed. Use the current session's presentation tools or local `python-pptx`. The optional helper `scripts/build_ppt.py` builds from an authored JSON plan. Read [slide-plan.md](references/slide-plan.md): academic cover/outline/content pages, titled panels, source images, native tables/flows, simple equations and structured notes are supported. Use `design.preset: academic-blue` for the bundled academic layout; the legacy generic helper remains useful for drafts and compatibility. Save `slide_plan.json` and `evidence.json`. Adapt or directly edit native objects when the source needs a composition beyond the helper.
5. Read [quality control](references/quality.md). Run `scripts/validate_ppt.py presentation.pptx --plan slide_plan.json --evidence evidence.json`, then render all slides and inspect the complete talk. Compare representative page types with the visual reference and fix failures in hierarchy, composition, legibility, alignment or finish. A generic text-and-image draft or passing structure report is insufficient as the finished deliverable. Record structural, evidence and visual/reference checks separately in QA, naming the renderer and any unverified checks. Automatic checks cannot establish scientific accuracy or all clipping/overlaps; do not claim full-paper coverage when material is incomplete.
